Tab Space vs Safari Tab Groups

Safari has built-in Tab Groups, so why would anyone use a tab-saving extension? Because the two solve different problems. Tab Groups keep tabs open; Tab Space lets you save your tabs, close them, and reopen them later.

Two different mental models

Safari Tab Groups are persistent workspaces. Every group stays alive: its tabs remain part of your browser, synced everywhere, always one click away — and always there. If you are the kind of person who keeps a handful of tidy, long-lived groups, they work great.

Tab Space follows the save & close model (the workflow OneTab made popular). When you finish a research spree with 30 tabs open, you press one shortcut: the whole window is stored as a named, taggable session and the tabs are gone. Safari gets fast and quiet again. Next week — or next year — you reopen the session, all tabs or just one.

Many Tab Space users tried Tab Groups first and came back. Not because Tab Groups are bad, but because a pile of one-off browsing sessions doesn't want to live as thirty permanent groups in a sidebar.

Side by side

Safari Tab Groups Tab Space
Core model Tabs stay open in persistent groups Save a snapshot, close the tabs, reopen anytime
Best for A few long-lived workspaces Many one-off sessions: research, shopping, trip planning, reading queues
Capture speed Move tabs into a group manually One click / one shortcut saves the whole window (or all windows)
Organization Group names Names, tags, favorites, search across titles, URLs and tags
History of past sessions No — a group is only its current state Yes — every save is kept, with automatic backups and export (Text, Markdown, HTML, JSON)
Browser weight All grouped tabs remain part of Safari Saved sessions live outside Safari; closed tabs cost nothing
Sync iCloud iCloud (Mac app + iPhone/iPad app)
Extras — Safari shortcuts (close duplicates, close same-domain tabs, copy URL as Markdown…), OneTab import, merge sessions
Price Free, built into Safari Paid, one-time purchase on the Mac App Store

When you should just use Tab Groups

  • You keep a small, stable set of workspaces ("Work", "Personal") and rarely archive anything.
  • You want tabs to stay live and loaded, not stored away.
  • You don't need tags, search, exports, or a history of past sessions.

When Tab Space fits better

  • You regularly end up with dozens of tabs you're not done with, but don't want open.
  • You think in sessions: "yesterday's bug hunt", "flights to Tokyo", "gift ideas".
  • You want one shortcut that saves everything and clears the window.
  • You want your saved tabs searchable, taggable, exportable — and backed up automatically.
  • You want the same library on your iPhone and iPad.
